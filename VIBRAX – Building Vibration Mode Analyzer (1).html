⚙️ How does it work?

Five simple steps, the same ones our program follows:

You enter

3 floor masses (kg) and 3 stiffness values (N/m).

Make two tables (matrices)

Mass matrix M says how heavy each floor is. Stiffness matrix K says how floors pull on each other.

Solve the magic equation

We look for special motions that keep their shape while wobbling:

K · v = λ · M · v
Get frequencies

ω = √λ (rad/s) and f = ω ÷ 2π (swings per second).

Draw the modes

Each eigenvector v is the pattern: how far each floor moves compared to the others.

📖 Tiny dictionary

Mass (m)
How heavy a floor is. Heavy things are lazy to move.
Stiffness (k)
How strongly the columns push back when bent, like a stiff spring.
Natural frequency
How many back-and-forth swings per second the building likes to do on its own.
Mode shape (eigenvector)
The pattern of movement: which floors go left, which go right, and by how much.
Resonance
If shaking matches a natural frequency, motion grows big, like pushing a swing at just the right moment.

Honest note: this is a simplified 3-floor model. It helps engineers understand vibration behaviour and design for earthquakes. It cannot alone say whether a real building will survive one.
